What is Hexadecimal?

Hexadecimal (often shortened to Hex) is a Base-16 number system.

  • Decimal: Base-10 (Digits 0-9). 10 digits total.
  • Binary: Base-2 (Digits 0-1). 2 digits total.
  • Hexadecimal: Base-16. Needs 16 digits!

We use 0-9, then we use letters A-F to represent values 10-15.

Decimal to Hexadecimal

Method: Repeated Division by 16.

1. Divide the decimal number by 16.
2. Write down the Remainder.
3. Divide the answer (quotient) by 16 again.
4. Repeat until the quotient is 0.
5. Read the remainders from Bottom to Top.

Example: Convert 20010 to Hex

200 ÷ 16 = 12 Remainder 8
12 ÷ 16 = 0 Remainder 12 (which is C in Hex)

Reading bottom to top: C8

20010 = C816

Hexadecimal to Decimal

Method: Multiplying by Powers of 16.

1. Write out the Hex digits.
2. Assign powers of 16 (16^0, 16^1, 16^2...).
3. Multiply the Hex digit value by the power.
4. Add the results together.

Example: Convert 2A16 to Decimal

Digits: 2 and A (Value 10)

Position of '2': 161 (16) → 2 × 16 = 32

Position of 'A': 160 (1) → 10 × 1 = 10

Total: 32 + 10 = 42

2A16 = 4210

Binary to Hexadecimal (Crucial for IGCSE)

This is the most common conversion in Computing. We group bits into "nibbles" (4 bits).

Method:

  1. Group the binary digits into sets of 4, starting from the RIGHT.
  2. If the last group on the left has fewer than 4 bits, add leading zeros.
  3. Convert each 4-bit group (nibble) to its Hex digit equivalent.

Hexadecimal to Binary

Method: Reverse of the previous slide.

  1. Write down the 4-bit binary equivalent for each Hex digit.
  2. Combine them together.
  3. Remove any leading zeros (optional, but good practice).

Challenge: Why is Hexadecimal used to represent MAC addresses (e.g., 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E)?

Answer: It represents 48 bits (6 bytes) in a compact, human-readable format that is easier to memorize or dictate than 48 individual 0s and 1s.
