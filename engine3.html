Engineering Design - Unit 3: Design

Lesson: Concept Generation & Selection
Overview

Lesson Objectives

🎯 Session 1 (45 min)

  • Understand the importance of ideation in the Engineering Design Process.
  • Differentiate between Criteria (goals) and Constraints (limitations).
  • Learn how to use a Decision Matrix to select the best design.

🛠 Session 2 (45 min)

  • Apply brainstorming techniques to generate multiple solutions.
  • Collaboratively evaluate designs using a weighted matrix.
  • Justify the final design selection.

Materials Needed: Sticky notes, whiteboard/markers, Projector, printed homework sheets.

Engage

Starter Activity: The "Crazy" Design

▶
Video: "Worst Product Designs Ever"

Discussion Question (5 min):

Why do you think these products failed? Did they fail because of bad ideas or because they didn't meet user needs?

Teacher Note: Guide students toward the concept that having a design is not enough; it must meet criteria and constraints.

Explore

Generating Concepts

Before we can select a design, we need options. Engineering is not about your first idea; it's about your best idea.

Mini-Activity: "100 Uses for a Paperclip"

You have 2 minutes. Write down as many uses for a paperclip as possible.

  • Rule 1: No judgment. (Crazy ideas are welcome).
  • Rule 2: Build on others' ideas.
  • Rule 3: Quantity over quality.

After timer: Ask students to share their wildest idea. Explain that this divergent thinking is crucial in Step 3 of the Engineering Design Process.

Explain

Criteria vs. Constraints

To evaluate designs fairly, we must define the rules of the game.

🎯 Criteria

What the design must do to be successful. These are the goals.

Example: The bridge must hold 10kg. The app must be easy to use.

⛓️ Constraints

Limitations placed on the design. Time, money, materials, physics.

Example: Must cost under $50. Must be built in 2 days. Only use cardboard.

Explain

The Decision Matrix (Pugh Chart)

How do we pick the winner when we have 3 good ideas? We use data. A Decision Matrix compares concepts against your criteria.

Criteria Weight (1-5) Idea A Idea B
Durability 5 (High) 3 (Okay) 5 (Great)
Cost 3 (Med) 4 (Cheap) 2 (Expensive)
Total Score - 27 31 (Winner)

Calculation: (Rating x Weight) = Score. Sum the scores.

Extend

Class Exercise: The Water Bottle Challenge

Scenario: Design a water bottle for hikers.

Criteria & Constraints:

  • Must hold at least 500ml (Criterion).
  • Must fit in a backpack side pocket (Constraint).
  • Must keep water cold for 4 hours (Criterion).

Task (15 min):

  1. Individually: Sketch 2 distinct bottle designs.
  2. Pair up: Exchange sketches.
  3. Partner: Fill out a mini-matrix for your partner's designs to decide which one they should build.
